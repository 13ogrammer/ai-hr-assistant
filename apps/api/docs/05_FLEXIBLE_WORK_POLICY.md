**Effective Date:** 1 July 2015  
**Last Updated:** 1 November 2026  
**Policy Review Date:** 1 November 2027

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the framework for flexible work arrangements at Pinnacle Solutions Pty Ltd, supporting work-life balance, employee wellbeing, and retention while maintaining operational effectiveness. The company is committed to considering flexible work requests and supporting varied work arrangements.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all full-time, part-time, and fixed-term employees. Casual employees may access some arrangements based on operational feasibility.

---

## 3. Legislative Framework

This policy implements the "Right to Request Flexible Working" under the Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth):

- All eligible employees have a legal right to request flexible arrangements
- Company must genuinely consider requests
- Refusal must be on reasonable grounds only
- Dismissal for requesting flexible work is prohibited

**Eligibility:**
- 12 months continuous service, or
- Part-time employee employed for 12+ months

## 4. Types of Flexible Arrangements

### 4.1 Flexible Hours

**Description:** Variable start/finish times within business requirements

**Example Arrangements:**
- 7:00 AM - 3:00 PM (instead of 9:00 AM - 5:00 PM)
- 9:30 AM - 5:30 PM (later start, later finish)
- Flexible core hours (must be present 10 AM - 3 PM, flexible outside)

**Suitability:**
- Most roles with customer/manager availability compatible
- Not available for roles requiring fixed coverage
- May require coordination with team

**Process:**
- Discuss with manager
- Specify required hours and flexibility needed
- Confirm core coverage hours
- Trial period (typically 4 weeks) before confirmation

### 4.2 Compressed Work Week

**Description:** Full-time hours compressed into fewer days

**Example Arrangements:**
- 4-day week (38 hours over 4 days): 9.5-10 hours per day
- 4-and-half-day week (38 hours): 4 days full, 1 day half-day
- Rotating compressed schedules

**Suitability:**
- Knowledge work without full-time coverage requirement
- Client-facing roles with structured availability
- Support roles where daily presence not critical

**Considerations:**
- Fatigue management important
- Rostering complexity
- Team coordination required

### 4.3 Part-Time Working

**Description:** Reduction from full-time to regular part-time hours

**Standard Arrangements:**
- 30 hours per week (3 days)
- 28 hours per week (3.5 days)
- 25 hours per week (2.5 days)
- Other arrangements by agreement

**Eligibility:**
- Typically available to full-time employees on application
- Financial and operational factors considered
- Performance expectations adjusted proportionally

**Changes to Part-Time:**
- May be permanent or temporary
- Temporary arrangement: 12-month maximum, then review
- Return to full-time possible if role available

### 4.4 Remote/Work-From-Home

**Description:** Working outside office for some/all days

**Arrangements:**
- **Full remote:** 5 days per week (rare, specific roles)
- **Hybrid:** Mix of office and home (e.g., 2 days office, 3 days home)
- **Occasional remote:** 1-2 days per week
- **Ad hoc:** Remote as needed for specific circumstances

**Eligibility Factors:**
- Role nature (not requiring daily office presence)
- Technology requirements met
- Employee's home setup adequate
- Team collaboration feasible
- Trust and communication capability

**Home Office Support:**
- Employee responsible for appropriate home workspace
- Company may provide equipment (laptop, monitor, chair) or stipend
- Security and confidentiality requirements apply
- Occupational health & safety standards must be met

### 4.5 Job Sharing

**Description:** Two part-time employees sharing one full-time role

**Arrangements:**
- Typically two 0.6 FTE employees (50-50 split)
- May be 60-40 or other split if operationally feasible
- Overlap days for handover/communication
- Shared responsibility for role

**Requirements:**
- Both employees must have compatible schedule
- Communication and coordination essential
- Single manager/reporting line for role
- May not suit all roles

### 4.6 Career Break or Sabbatical

**Description:** Extended unpaid leave followed by return

**Arrangements:**
- 3 months to 12 months unpaid leave
- Position held or equivalent role upon return
- No break in service (for benefits calculation)
- Advance planning required

**Eligibility:**
- Strong performer, minimum 3 years service preferred
- Operational coverage arranged
- Return date agreed in writing
- Superannuation contribution negotiable

### 4.7 Phased Retirement

**Description:** Gradual transition to retirement via reduced hours

**Arrangements:**
- Reduction to 0.6-0.8 FTE from age 55+ (typically)
- Gradual reduction over 1-3 years
- Flexible timing of hours
- Potential Transition to Retirement Income Stream (if superannuation available)

**Benefits:**
- Maintains continuity of knowledge
- Mentoring of junior staff possible
- Gradual financial transition for employee

## 5. Flexible Work Request Process

### 5.1 Making a Request

**Step 1: Prepare Request**
- Consider what flexibility needed and why
- Identify specific arrangement(s) that would help
- Draft proposal including:
  - Type of arrangement
  - Proposed hours/timing
  - Start date
  - Duration (temporary or permanent)
  - How work would be managed
  - Any impacts on role or team

**Step 2: Submit Request**
- Submit written request to direct manager
- Include all relevant details
- Request meeting to discuss

**Step 3: Initial Discussion**
- Manager meets with employee within 5 business days
- Discuss arrangement feasibility
- Explore options and concerns
- Take notes for formal consideration

**Step 4: Formal Consideration**
- Manager consults with:
  - HR department
  - Team members (if appropriate)
  - Relevant stakeholders
  - Operational planning
- Formal decision made within 2-4 weeks

**Step 5: Decision**
- Manager meets with employee
- Decision provided in writing
- If approved: implementation date and trial period
- If not approved: reasons provided and alternatives discussed

### 5.2 Approval Criteria

Request will be **approved** if:

1. **Operational feasibility:**
   - Role can function effectively with new arrangement
   - Team coverage maintained
   - Client/customer needs met
   - Business continuity assured

2. **Performance capability:**
   - Employee capable of managing flexible arrangement
   - Quality of work maintained
   - Responsiveness to business needs
   - Reliability and accountability

3. **No unreasonable cost:**
   - Implementation costs reasonable
   - No significant additional expense
   - Technology/tools available

4. **Fairness:**
   - Arrangement not unfairly advantaging one employee
   - Consistent with policies and precedents

### 5.3 Refusal Grounds

Request may be **refused** on these reasonable grounds:

1. **Operational impact:** Significant business disruption
2. **Cost:** Unreasonable additional cost to implement
3. **Workload impact:** Materially adverse impact on team
4. **Quality:** Cannot maintain service/product quality
5. **Health & Safety:** Arrangement creates unmanageable OHS risk
6. **Customer impact:** Materially adverse effect on customer/client
7. **Contractual:** Existing constraints preventing arrangement

**Refusal Notice:**
- Must be in writing
- Must specify reason(s)
- Must offer alternative arrangements (if any)
- Right to appeal (see Section 5.5)

### 5.4 Trial Period

Approved arrangements typically operate on trial:

**Duration:** 4-8 weeks initial trial (agreed in advance)

**During Trial:**
- Regular check-ins with manager (weekly or bi-weekly)
- Focus on operational impact, quality, communication
- Adjustment negotiations if needed
- Feedback from team/stakeholders

**At Trial End:**
- Formal review meeting
- Decision: confirm permanent, extend trial, or cease
- Written confirmation

---

## 5.5 Appeals Process

If request denied:

1. **Appeal Notification:**
   - Employee notifies HR Manager in writing within 10 business days
   - Specifies reasons for disagreement with decision
   - Proposes alternative arrangement if applicable

2. **Appeal Meeting:**
   - HR Manager meets with employee and original decision-maker
   - New considerations heard
   - Compromise arrangements explored
   - Meeting held within 2 weeks of notice

3. **Appeal Decision:**
   - Final decision made by HR Manager
   - Provided in writing within 5 business days
   - Includes reasons and any alternative arrangements
   - This constitutes final internal decision

4. **External Resolution:**
   - If employee unsatisfied, may lodge claim with Fair Work Commission
   - Free mediation service available
   - Union support available if applicable

## 6. Flexible Arrangement Agreements

### 6.1 Formal Documentation

Upon approval, a written Flexible Work Arrangement Agreement is created:

**Covers:**
- Type of arrangement
- Days/hours/pattern
- Reporting and communication requirements
- Coverage/team coordination details
- How leave is taken and accrues
- Pay implications (if any)
- Review and termination clauses
- Expectations during arrangement

### 6.2 Variation of Arrangement

Once agreed, arrangement may be varied:

- Either party can request variation with reasonable notice
- Changes to business requirements accommodated
- Employee circumstances changes discussed
- Mutually agreed variations documented

### 6.3 Termination of Arrangement

Arrangement may cease:

- By mutual agreement
- Due to operational change (restructure, client loss, etc.)
- Due to performance issues related to arrangement
- By employee choice (return to full-time)
- By company if arrangement not working (with notice period)

**Notice Period:** Minimum 4 weeks notice for termination

## 7. Pay and Benefits During Flexible Arrangements

### 7.1 Pay Adjustments

**Part-time arrangements:** Pay reduced proportionally to hours

- Example: 0.6 FTE receives 60% of full-time salary
- Allowances and loadings applied proportionally
- Superannuation calculated on reduced salary

**Remote/flexible hours arrangements:** No pay change

- Same hours, same pay
- No reduction for location change
- All benefits continue

### 7.2 Leave Accrual

**Annual leave:** Accrues based on hours worked

- Part-time: 4 weeks pro-rata
- Flexible full-time: Full 4 weeks annual

**Personal leave:** Accrues based on hours worked

- Part-time: 10 days pro-rata
- Flexible full-time: Full 10 days

**Long service leave:** Accrues on continuous service basis

- Unaffected by flexible arrangement

### 7.3 Superannuation

- Company contributes 11.5% of ordinary time earnings
- Calculated on actual ordinary hours worked
- Employee voluntary contributions possible
- Defined contribution fund

## 8. Performance and Expectations

### 8.1 Performance Standards

Flexible arrangement does NOT reduce performance expectations:

- Work quality remains high
- Deadlines met
- Responsiveness maintained
- Professional standards upheld
- Productivity equivalent to full-time arrangement

### 8.2 Management and Accountability

**Communication:**
- Regular check-ins with manager
- Clear work priorities and deadlines
- Availability during agreed hours
- Prompt response to urgent matters

**Responsiveness:**
- Flexible staff must be available for urgent matters (even outside agreed hours)
- Escalation procedures understood
- Coverage arrangements respected

### 8.3 Review and Feedback

Flexible arrangements reviewed:

- At end of trial period (formal)
- Annually (informal)
- Performance reviews normal process (expectations adjusted proportionally for part-time)
- Feedback given on arrangement effectiveness

---

## 9. Team and Client Considerations

### 9.1 Team Communication

When flexible arrangement approved:

- Team informed of new arrangement
- Coverage and handover procedures explained
- Changes to meetings/availability communicated
- Support for collaboration maintained

### 9.2 Client/Customer Facing Roles

For roles with external contact:

- Availability during client hours maintained
- Coverage during remote periods confirmed
- Contact details/escalation points clear
- Client satisfaction maintained

---

## 10. Special Circumstances

### 10.1 Parental Leave and Return

- Returning parents strongly encouraged to discuss flexible arrangements
- Can be part of return-to-work plan
- Gradual return to full-time often accommodated
- Arrangements reviewed as child care established

### 10.2 Carer Responsibilities

- Flexible arrangements available for those with carer duties
- Aging parent care, disability care supported
- Additional leave beyond personal leave sometimes discussed

### 10.3 Study or Professional Development

- Employees studying part-time may access flexible arrangements
- Reduced hours during intensive study periods possible
- Professional development (conferences, training) accommodated via leave

### 10.4 Health Conditions

- Chronic health conditions supported through flexibility
- Medical evidence may be requested
- Arrangements adjusted as health improves/changes
- Occupational health & safety assessment possible

## 11. Occupational Health & Safety

### 11.1 Remote Working Safety

Company ensures:

- OHS risk assessment for remote setup
- Employee training on home office ergonomics
- Support for appropriate furniture/equipment
- Regular check-ins on wellbeing
- Incident reporting procedures apply

### 11.2 Wellbeing Monitoring

- Managers monitor isolation risks for remote workers
- Regular team contact maintained
- Mental health support offered
- Social connection opportunities provided
